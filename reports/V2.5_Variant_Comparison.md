## Workload observations

Both builders measured an appointment read returning two visits and a reminder batch before and after adding 600 older records. In the performance-only build the read stayed at 6 statements and 5 instantiated records, and the batch stayed at 26 statements and 5 instantiated records. In the loop build the read stayed at 7 statements and 6 instantiated records, and the batch stayed at 29 statements and 15 instantiated records. The loop build added older attempts as well as visits and committed a different number of results. Treat each as a growth check within its build. The figures support both builds' history-resistance on these fixtures. Different work and fixtures limit what the absolute counts and timings say head to head.

The additional grading rounds did yield concrete verification and repairs, including safe use of a shared lock on a fresh or upgraded database. V2.5.1.0.0 still had the better structure in both blind reviews. This is one paired rebuild and cannot establish the average effect of the loop.

### Transactions, rejection, and late failure

A locks the clinic across refresh and dependent writes. `PmsRefresh` opens a savepoint, reads a local-date slice plus tracked future scheduled IDs, rejects missing tracked rows, upserts changed contacts and visits, retires superseded work, and records sync status. Rejection rolls back the savepoint and records `sync_error` in the caller’s transaction. The reminder batch then stops on refresh rejection or loads context, plans simulated sends and work, and flushes its buffers. Ownership failure rolls back refresh and batch changes before saving the error under the outer lock. A reply validates address/thread and checks replay identity under the lock. Exact SMS `STOP` writes the opt-out before refresh is needed. Other replies refresh, match history, check ownership, safety and revision, then confirm and resolve no-reply work or record review with a reason. A late raised write failure rolls back the enclosing workflow. See A `pms_refresh.rb` lines 16–68, `reminder_run.rb` lines 8–45, and `inbound_reply.rb` lines 4–70.

B reads versioned PMS records directly. `ClinicState.with_replica` owns repeatable read, locks and advances the clinic generation, and retries serialization failures up to three total attempts. The batch reads scheduled visits, takes a shared contact-directory lock, loads context, reconciles open work, processes visits, and stamps success. Its source-unavailable handler stamps failure after rollback. The source probe only establishes that a joined appointment exists, so a partial disappearance can look like a deletion during reconciliation. Replies pass that probe even for duplicates and `STOP`, then resolve thread or current address, reject ambiguous SMS origins, and opt out, confirm, or write review. Review requires an appointment anchor, so a disappeared anchor can turn review into a 404. Late exceptions roll back local writes. Neither fake adapter demonstrates external delivery atomicity. A’s report action broadly maps statement failures to 503; B globally maps connection failures to PMS unavailability, including failures whose origin could be local. See B `clinic_state.rb` lines 6–34, `reminder_batch.rb` lines 13–54, `patient_reply.rb` lines 15–72, and both controller rescue definitions.

A’s `ConcurrentRemindersTest` S15 asserts concurrent counts `[0, 1]`, one stored message, and zero sends on retry. A’s S03 asserts stale YES enters review with the new revision still unconfirmed. B’s “a replacement visit at a new number never receives a YES or STOP sent from the old number” asserts rejection of ambiguous input, old-number-only opt-out, and confirmation of the replacement from its own number. These assertions provide coverage evidence. No complete supplied test asserts rollback after a late write failure.

### Moving the first reminder earlier

In A, change `FIRST_DAY` from seven to eight. It drives both candidate selection and the initial catch-up window. The existing PMS window already reaches day eight. Keep the revision/step key and day-six backup, including immediate fallback after SMS failure. In B, extend `LOOKAHEAD_DAYS`; `next_step` derives the initial window from that query and the remaining thresholds. The same constant bounds reply candidates, so day-eight sends need a same-day confirmation case. Preserve B’s local-day suppression and later backup. See A `reminder_run.rb` lines 2–6, 34–36, 92–122, 147–153 and B `reminder_batch.rb` lines 2–6, 17–18, 92–135 and `patient_reply.rb` lines 45–52. Test no initial on day nine, one on day eight, repeat and catch-up runs, backup and follow-up timing, and final sends across a clinic-local date boundary. A’s S05 and B’s caught-up initial-text test assert their different fallback behavior.

### Growth and React state

Irrelevant appointment history is mostly excluded by date predicates. A also reads tracked scheduled future IDs beyond its refresh window to detect missing records. B’s reconciliation targets open non-review work, while both boards count clinic-wide work despite paginating results. Reply matching deliberately retains historical recipient versions. `distinct.limit(2)` bounds returned versions while preserving ambiguity detection, with database effort unmeasured. As eligible work grows, A materializes the source slice, reloads contact IDs after upsert, and batches writes under the clinic lock. B materializes visits and attempts, repeats time conversion, and performs per-visit writes while holding clinic and shared directory locks. B’s independent ID and revision filters can admit mismatched pairs if revisions overlap, and its batch groups by ID alone.

Both keep filters and drafts near their pages. A explicitly retains the reply draft and event key after failure, then resets both after success. B delegates request identity and pending state to `useWrite`, refreshes on success, and keeps the selected origin. Hook implementations, schema and PMS view definitions, omitted screens and tests, and runtime measurements are outside the evidence.

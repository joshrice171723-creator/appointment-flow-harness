### Routes and ownership

Work items map to `index/show/update`, contact detail and verification to `show/update`, and nested follow-ups to `create` → `StaffFollowUp`. POST `demo_sessions` establishes the signed identity. POST `reminder_runs` calls `ReminderRun`, which refreshes internally. Nested clinic/patient reads map to `DemoPatients#index` and `DemoMessages#index`, and replies to `DemoReplies#create` → `InboundReply`. `FrontDeskController` derives the clinic from the token. `FrontDeskView` sits among services, and the reply controller borrows message rendering from another controller. (`routes.rb:8–25`, `front_desk_controller.rb:6–17`, `demo_replies_controller.rb:8–14`)
- **B** namespaces `Api` and nests staff resources under `/api/clinics/{clinic_id}`. The singular `pms_refresh` and `reminder_batch` POSTs call `PmsSync` and `ReminderBatch`. Separate demo clinic routes expose patient `index/show` and reply `create` → `ReplyIntake`. Workspaces use `index`, staff session `create`, and leadership report `show`. The stored staff session must match the route clinic. (`routes.rb:3–22`, `api/staff_controller.rb:8–20`)
- **C** uses clinic `index`, nested dashboard `show` → `WorkBoard`, appointments `index` → `AppointmentOverview`, and patients `index/show`. Assignment, follow-up, verification, and reply POSTs map to `create` → `StaffFollowUp`, `VerifyPatientContact`, or `PatientReply`. ### Workflow and failure behavior

**A.** Under the clinic lock, refresh reads a date window plus tracked upcoming IDs, rejects incomplete results, upserts changed contacts and visits, retires obsolete work, and stamps success. Refresh rejection rolls back its savepoint and records an error. Reminder processing loads batch context, plans fake sends, then writes messages and work. Late ownership failure rolls back refresh and reminder changes before recording the error. Replies validate and deduplicate under the lock. STOP records opt-out immediately. Other replies refresh, match delivery versions, then confirm and close work or save a review with a reason. Stale staff selections preserve the completed refresh. HTTP translation is mostly centralized, with reminder failures mapped separately. The broad refresh read rescue logs clinic and exception class. S06/S07 asserts matching retry identity, changed-payload rejection, and preserved confirmation after NO. (`pms_refresh.rb:16–29,38–67`, `reminder_run.rb:8–45`, `inbound_reply.rb:4–70`, `staff_follow_up.rb:20–43`, `confirmation_workflow_test.rb:69–78`)

**B.** `PmsSync` takes the clinic lock, reads a repeatable-read replica snapshot, updates changed rows, marks missing scheduled visits, and stamps status. `ReminderBatch` holds the lock through refresh, channel checks, fake delivery, and per-visit writes. Replies deduplicate, handle STOP, or refresh and reload the patient before matching and confirming or opening review. Unexpected late write failures roll back the outer operation. A stale staff outcome is rescued inside the lock so refresh persists. The controller maps typed invalid operations and false refresh results, while the replica read rescue groups database failures. S03 at lines 64–76 asserts that older requests on the same appointment still force review. The corresponding guard misses an old request on a **different** appointment once it ages out of the ten-day window. (`pms_sync.rb:4–52,73–103`, `reminder_batch.rb:8–14,47–91`, `reply_intake.rb:13–89`, `staff_follow_up.rb:13–21`)

**C.** Refresh means reading the replica directly. The batch locks clinic state in a repeatable-read transaction, loads scheduled visits, takes a shared directory lock, reconciles obsolete work, sends, and stamps success. Even STOP requires the initial replica probe. Other replies match the email thread or recipient history, then record a decision or review. Rejection and late exceptions roll back transaction writes, including earlier candidate decisions. Serialization failures retry up to three total attempts. Recognized batch source failure is stamped outside the transaction. The global connection rescue can label a local database failure as PMS downtime. The replacement-number test asserts old-number review, old-number-only STOP, and confirmation of the replacement via the new number. (`clinic_state.rb:4–34`, `reminder_batch.rb:13–39`, `patient_reply.rb:15–71`, `api/application_controller.rb:2–19`, `reminder_workflow_test.rb:59–78`)

### Growth, revision identity, and the schedule change

A and B start revisions at `1` and increment per visit. Overlap is expected, and visit IDs remain part of message identity. A’s SQL joins attempts to each visit’s current revision. B preloads all deliveries for eligible visits and filters revisions in Ruby. C uses independent ID and revision predicates, but its revision hash includes the globally unique appointment ID. Cross-appointment matches therefore require a hash collision under the supplied invariant. Same-row values can recur if all digest inputs recur, including nullable `updated_at`. (`A create_appointment_workflow.rb:25–57`, `pms_refresh.rb:85–115`, `B create_confirmation_workspace.rb:30–60`, `pms_sync.rb:101–103`, `C version_replica_appointments.rb:4–9`)

B’s refresh materializes historically linked patients and all local clinic patients. Eligible growth adds ownership and opt-out reads and individual writes under lock. A bounds its projection and batches writes, though address matching has no supplied expression indexes. C avoids projection writes and reconciles open work in SQL while preserving reply reviews. It still materializes eligible visits and writes per visit under both locks. A and C deliberately consult historical delivery identities for ambiguous replies. A two-version limit bounds returned identities, with database work unmeasured. (`B pms_sync.rb:35–69`, `A contact_address_ownership.rb:24–40`, `C reminder_batch.rb:22–30,44–53,68–120`)

Move A’s `FIRST_DAY` and B’s `INITIAL_DAYS` to eight, covering A’s date window and B’s valid and invalid time windows. In C, extend `LOOKAHEAD_DAYS` or split the horizons explicitly. `PatientReply` also uses it, so day-eight replies must still match. Test day nine versus eight across a clinic-local date boundary, same-day reruns, existing initial attempts, backup timing, follow-up, final sends, and immediate day-eight replies. Preserve A’s immediate failed-SMS fallback and B/C’s same-day suppression. (`C reminder_batch.rb:2–17,92–98,123–135`, `patient_reply.rb:45–52`)

C’s React excerpts show URL-driven desk selection and local page state, with a read-only board and conversation writes delegated to `useWrite`. Hook retry semantics are unseen. A’s most material weakness is that follow-up can overwrite assignment despite the assignment endpoint’s conflict guard. B’s is the cross-appointment reply gap. C’s is the unverified reminder invocation and directory-lock setup. Evidence for C’s real entry point, directory schema, and concurrent rollback and retry behavior would most change the ranking. (`C main.tsx:65–100`, `inbox.tsx:47–75`, `A work_items_controller.rb:27–35`, `staff_follow_up.rb:20–39`)

### Routes and workflow behavior

All paths below are under `/api`. **A** uses flat controllers and derives staff clinic scope from a signed demo token (`front_desk_controller.rb:6–13`). `POST /reminder_runs` reaches `ReminderRuns#create` → `ReminderRun` → `PmsRefresh`. `GET /appointments`, `/work_items`, `/contacts/:id`, and `/reports` reach their read actions. Work-item and contact updates own assignment and verification respectively. Nested clinic/patient message GETs and reply POSTs go to demo controllers, with `InboundReply` owning intake (`routes.rb:8–22`). The base controller translates parameter and domain rejection to 422. Reports separately translate any `StatementInvalid` to 503.

**B** nests staff resources under `/clinics/:clinic_id`. `POST /pms_refresh` and `/reminder_batch` reach their respective `create` actions and services. Appointments and work items use `index`, assignment uses `work_items#update`, patient detail uses `show`, and nested verification and follow-ups use `update` and `create`. Demo patient reads and reply creation sit under `/demo_clinics/:demo_clinic_id/patients`, with `ReplyIntake` owning the write (`routes.rb:7–21`). The staff session establishes clinic scope (`staff_controller.rb:8–15`). Controllers translate service-specific validation and source failure.

**C** nests dashboard `show`, appointment `index`, patient `index/show`, inbox `show`, and assignment, follow-up, verification, and reply `create` under clinics. `WorkBoard`, `AppointmentOverview`, `StaffFollowUp`, `VerifyPatientContact`, and `PatientReply` make the corresponding rules findable. Staff actions check the clinic’s demo staff header. The routes expose sync status through reads. The entry point invoking `ReminderBatch.run` is outside the excerpts (`routes.rb:4–18`, `clinic_controller.rb:7–14`).

**A** locks the clinic through refresh, planning, fake delivery, and persistence. Refresh reads a date window plus tracked upcoming IDs, rejects missing tracked rows, and upserts changed contacts and visits. Source rejection records a sync error. An ownership lookup failure rolls back the inner reminder transaction, including refresh, before recording the error. Other late write exceptions propagate and roll back. Reply intake checks replay, handles STOP before refresh, then matches history and either confirms or writes review, under the same clinic lock. The source ownership lookup can examine unrelated PMS patients through normalization expressions, while current-revision joins keep old attempts out of batch materialization. The biggest unresolved weakness is that reply review stores candidate IDs and later validates a revision supplied from the current work detail, losing the original candidate revision (`pms_refresh.rb:16–29,34–101`, `reminder_run.rb:8–20,50–60`, `inbound_reply.rb:36–70,109–114`, `staff_follow_up.rb:53–59`).

**B** holds the clinic lock across a repeatable-read source snapshot, local reconciliation, and dependent writes. It saves changed rows individually. Missing scheduled visits in the window are superseded and flagged. A batch refreshes first, then iterates candidates, checking shared addresses and opt-outs per visit, filtering preloaded deliveries by revision, and writing attempts and work. Reply intake checks replay and STOP, refreshes, then confirms or reviews. Source rejection records an error and lets intake save a review. Uncaught late write failures unwind local writes. Stale staff outcomes are caught inside the lock so refresh can commit before rejection. The broad source rescue covers `StatementInvalid`. The biggest unresolved behavioral weakness is that an old unthreaded reply can become apparently unique after ten days when the new request belongs to a different visit (`pms_sync.rb:16–52,55–103`, `reminder_batch.rb:26–91`, `reply_intake.rb:13–89`, `staff_follow_up.rb:13–21`).

**C** locks a clinic-state row in repeatable read and advances its generation on commit, with bounded serialization retries. Batch and reply take a shared contact-directory lock through commit. Batch loads scheduled visits by clinic and date, preloads matching state, retires obsolete open work in SQL, then writes per eligible attempt. Reply checks replay and origin, records STOP or confirms a matching current visit, otherwise saves a review. Late exceptions roll back workflow writes. Batch source rejection records a failure timestamp outside the lock, which can race with a newer success. The biggest weakness is the availability probe itself. One appointment with a patient can pass it even when other appointments are missing, and reconciliation can then retire their work. The base controller also maps connection-establishment errors generally to PMS unavailability (`clinic_state.rb:6–33`, `reminder_batch.rb:13–53`, `patient_reply.rb:15–72`, `application_controller.rb:2–4`).

### Revision identity and change check

A and B use per-visit integers starting at 1, so revisions routinely overlap across visits. Their delivery checks preserve visit identity alongside revision. C’s independent ID and revision predicates look broader, but `create_replica.rb:23–33` makes appointment ID a primary key and `version_replica_appointments.rb:4–9` hashes it into the revision. Cross-appointment overlap would require a hash collision under that generator. Identical inputs can recur on one appointment, so the digest provides identity without chronological ordering.

Move A’s `FIRST_DAY` and B’s `INITIAL_DAYS` from 7 to 8. A’s local-date range and B’s instant and invalid-wall-time windows follow those constants. Their refresh windows already cover day eight. In C, extend the batch date range and explicitly cover replies to day-eight sends. Keep backup, follow-up, final, and existing failure fallback rules. Test no initial at day nine, one at day eight, retries on days eight and seven, and unchanged later steps across a clinic-local date boundary.

A’s `ConcurrentRemindersTest` S15 asserts one persisted send across two workers and zero on retry. C’s “caught-up initial text” test asserts same-day suppression and next-day backup. B’s “reschedule cannot make an unthreaded YES unambiguous” checks review after the window for the **same** visit. The visible tests do not establish late-write rollback across all three. React fetch cancellation and retry behavior also remain unverified because the API hooks are omitted.

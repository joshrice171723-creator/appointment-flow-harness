## Saved guidance for the builder

This section describes the guidance used in the frozen V2.6.1.1.0 experiment. The V2.7.1.1.0 follow-up below adds the controller, Active Record, and grading rules agreed after that build.

Before coding a consequential workflow, identify the business decision, the eligible data, what the caller needs back, and the transaction that protects the decision. Use the smallest useful result: `exists?` for a boolean, `pluck` or `pick` for scalar values, `select` for a narrow model result, `group(...).count` for totals, and scoped preloads for associations the caller reads. Reuse data already available within the correct snapshot. Batch similar writes when uniqueness, callbacks, timestamps, and rollback remain correct. Keep database-specific expressions clear and bind inputs. [Rails querying guide](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/v8.1/active_record_querying.html) · [Rails bulk write API](https://api.rubyonrails.org/classes/ActiveRecord/Relation.html#method-i-upsert_all)

The source should let someone follow the normal entry point through the business rule, transaction or lock, persisted result, and caller response. Use named private steps when they help a reader locate a meaningful decision. Preserve failures that callers need to distinguish and put required failure state where it can survive rollback. The AI verifies the relevant failure and retry cases.

## V2.7.1.1.0 follow-up

- Keep a controller action readable in execution order. Name private steps after the business or screen decision they own. Keep screen-specific queries, filters, and response assembly in the controller. Extract a service when actual callers share the operation or when it owns business rules and a transaction boundary that warrant independent testing.
- Use plain Active Record for application queries. Avoid handwritten SQL strings, SQL expressions, and Arel. Keep the full collection, any rule-specific subset, and the displayed rows distinct. Verify the endpoint's applicable clinic scope, date boundaries, filters, totals, order, limit, review flags, response fields, and query growth.
- Require evidence against a concrete failure for each grade. Start the review by challenging a material risk with weak coverage. Inspect the assertions in reused tests and tie evidence to the revision. A proven issue requires repair. Missing evidence leaves the grade open. The AI runs the checks, fixes the issue, and regrades.

V2.5.1.1.0 loads full `Message` models before checking attempt date, step, channel, and status (`ReminderBatch`, line 24). The same build shows a better pattern in `WorkBoard#page` (lines 18–23), which limits the page and fetches only IDs and names for its selected patients. The harness asks the builder to choose the fields used by the decision and verify object counts. A per-recipient write also appears in `ReminderBatch` (line 103). A bulk write earns its place if the measured gain survives the same uniqueness, failure, and retry rules.

Both builds perform multiple activity counts per clinic even though some totals are already grouped (`OutcomeReport` and `LeadershipReport`). The current fixture targets one clinic. Treat a many-clinic reporting benchmark as a separate question about supported scope. Keep the business result and clinic-local time rules steady when increasing data the operation could touch. [Active Record audit](V2.5.1.0.0_Active_Record_Audit.md) · [Matched V2.5 benchmark](V2.5_Database_Audit.md)

The matched growth test confirmed the distinction. V2.5.1.0.0 stayed at 26 statements when 600 later appointments were added, while Rails read 1,213 rows instead of 13 and affected 1,207 rows instead of seven. The same two messages were sent. V2.5.1.1.0 stayed at 18 statements, 10 rows returned and three rows affected. The earlier build also maintains a local calendar projection. A fair improvement needs to preserve the calendar behavior and check how much source and projection work repeats on a retry. ## How the candidate will be judged

- Run the same clean-room assignment with the existing external prompt, model, and effort. Keep prior source, known fixes, audit notes, and fixed comparison fixtures outside the builder's project.
- Checkpoint the runnable app before grading. Preserve the final revision, the checks it passed, and the findings that led to repairs.
- Use a separate comparison driver against frozen revisions with a fixed clock and known sends. Exercise a small workload, extra old history, unrelated patients, source appointments close enough to enter a broad refresh but too far out for a reminder, a larger batch, and a same-clock retry. Assert reminder counts and equivalent appointment-list results. Keep the raw trace and clearly label timing conditions.
- Check how another AI reads and changes an implemented rule, and record the affected production files and focused verification. Score readability separately from observed database work.

The candidate combines several instruction changes. The run can show how its output compares with the frozen references. Repeated matched trials would be needed to isolate the contribution of any one change.

# V2.5 harness starting-point decision

The original project baseline is `appointment-flow` (called V1 in earlier notes). The review below compares two standalone harness builds. See the [experiment dashboard](../dashboard/index.html) for the original project and the measured V2.5 variants.

V2.5.0.0.0 is the primary harness starting point. The blind source reviewer ranked it ahead of V2.4.1.1.0 for readability, changeability, and the likely growth of database work. Rails controller actions tied. V2.5.0.0.0 still holds a clinic lock through source I/O and reminder processing. Runtime performance was outside this comparison.

V2.5's verified application commit was e435b4d. Its archived review input f62d0e6 adds build and review evidence. The application passed eight checks, including 24 Rails tests with 140 assertions, two React tests, and three Edge scenarios. The candidate-authored decision file is pending.

V2.5 added guidance for planning a consequential workflow before implementation. The builder chose its PMS namespace during the first scaffold. This trial also used a standalone Codex launch, verified skills/hooks/plugins/memory off, and a pinned model and effort. The evidence establishes the output and its organization. The effect of the harness and the effect of the launch environment remain unresolved.

## Independent source review

**Sample A wins overall for readability, changeability, and how database work grows.** Its model separates patient contact, appointment revisions, deliveries, and staff work, and its first-reminder rule has one timing owner. Sample B gives read queries a clearer home and can run a batch against a recent snapshot, but its refresh and reply paths bring more historical data into Ruby.

The focused check for both belongs at `ReminderBatch.call` or `ReminderBatch#call`, with a fixed clock and stable contact. Use a late clinic-local appointment to verify no initial send nine days out, one eight days out, and no repeat on retry or at the former seven-day boundary. Then verify the existing backup, follow-up, and final sends under each sample’s eligibility rules. The supplied excerpts contain no test files to locate an existing check.

The decisive difference is that A keeps timing policy and revision identity easier to trace while narrowing the appointment and reply data it materializes. Its important weakness is the clinic-wide lock around source I/O and the entire reminder batch. As eligible visits and their delivery histories grow, unrelated writes for that clinic can wait behind the batch. These are source-based scaling judgments. The excerpts establish neither query plans nor measured costs.

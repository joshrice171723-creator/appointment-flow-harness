## New endpoint checks

**Calendar filters and totals.** The fixture had 201 same-day visits for one clinic, one visit just past the day boundary, and one other-clinic visit. One visit was confirmed. The fixture also included one open and one resolved reply review. Each tab returned HTTP 200 and kept the daily totals at 201 appointments, one confirmed, 200 unconfirmed, and one requiring review. The `all`, `confirmed`, `unconfirmed`, and `review` tabs returned 200, 1, 200, and 1 rows respectively. Each used eight reads. The test verified row order, the cap, patient names, and open-review flags.

**Aged-out unresolved work with a missing PMS row.** The first reminder request created a no-response work item for a next-day appointment. After advancing five days, the test removed that appointment from the synthetic replica and sent another authenticated request. The controller returned HTTP 201 and moved the successful-sync timestamp from November 16 to November 21. The old task stayed open and no additional reminders were recorded. The app currently filters these old open-work visits by whether the source row still exists before deciding which missing rows to detect. Any repair needs to define how this state is surfaced and preserve work without treating an appointment's disappearance as proof of an outcome.

The independent reviewer worked from frozen source excerpts, historical assertions, and benchmark receipts. The two endpoint checks and the revision comparison were performed separately and supply evidence the source-only reviewer did not have.
